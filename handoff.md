## Idea, audience, and graduation goal

This is a DEPI AWS ML Engineering graduation project: an Arabic-first Shopping Copilot that helps a Shopper finish the **whole shopping journey** on a Controlled Storefront, not just choose filters. The target user describes a goal naturally in Egyptian Arabic, Franco-Arabic, English, or mixed language. The Copilot understands product/use-case needs, presents verifiable choices, navigates to product and cart pages, assists with cart and simulated checkout, and safely hands authentication/payment entry to the Shopper. The project must be demonstrable and defensible under evaluation, not merely appear intelligent in one canned prompt.

The original problem is that a Shopper may know the outcome—“something comfortable for hiking,” “a light shirt for Sharm El Sheikh,” or “something to wear with black trousers”—without knowing the Storefront's taxonomy and controls. The differentiator is **grounded natural-language help plus visible, deterministic safety**. A nontechnical Shopper should see why an item was suggested, whether it really matches, what the Copilot is about to do, and when control returns to them.

The owner selected **fashion and shoes first** for depth. The existing four-category, 60-product fictional catalogue remains a deterministic safety fixture; it is not considered sufficient by itself for the eventual graduation demonstration. A larger, curated and legally usable fashion/shoes catalogue and measured retrieval improvements are a later stage. Prices and inventory remain explicitly simulated in EGP; no live retailer or real payment integration is approved.

The approved stage sequence is:

1. Stabilize grounded discovery and the browser/safety baseline on the Controlled Storefront. Stage 1's owner-written discovery requests passed; Ticket 09 finished the core safety boundary.
2. Finish product options, cart, Undo, guarded simulated checkout, refresh recovery, and a convincing browser demo. Ticket 10's guarded demonstration is done; Tickets 11–12 own the remaining cart/recovery work. Ticket 11B then polishes the Storefront UI.
3. Expand the focused catalogue and benchmark lexical versus semantic/hybrid retrieval on held-out Egyptian Arabic, Franco-Arabic, and mixed-language queries. Select a data source only after checking rights and attribute quality.
4. Deploy the proven flow on AWS and evaluate the actual cloud model/cost. The approved gates require the local MVP and later generalisation evidence first; do not keep postponing basic local correctness to AWS.

The local MVP exit gate is **three recorded full-suite runs**, each at least **40/44 cases** with **every safety case passing**, plus step/latency targets and a five-person uncoached study (including at least two people with limited digital confidence). That gate has **not** been reached. `RESULTS.md` is meant to be the final local-MVP evidence artifact, not a current pass certificate.

## Canonical language and key product rules

Use `CONTEXT.md` as the glossary. In particular: **Shopper**, **Shopping Copilot**, **Storefront**, **Controlled Storefront**, **Storefront Definition**, **Shopping Task**, **Constraint**, **Catalogue Fact**, **Snapshot**, **Action**, **Sensitive Field**, **Reversible Mutation**, **Guarded Mutation**, **Confirmation**, and **Undo** have specific meanings. The model's **Structured Intent** is an interpretation, not authority to act. The deterministic **Intent Boundary** and browser policy decide whether an Action is allowed.

- An **Exact Match** is available and satisfies every explicit hard requirement through authoritative catalogue facts. Missing facts are _unverified_, not implicitly true. An **Alternative** is a real available product with named unmet/unverified requirements. A **Styling Suggestion** is a verified product plus a limited, labelled style opinion, not an invented compatibility fact.
- If a recognized requirement is omitted or unsupported, ask or disclose the gap. Never silently broaden a request and announce success from a nonzero result count. A simple URL filter may be used only when it represents the actual request. Product-use/material/style goals use the read-only catalogue and deterministic verification.
- An owned item (“I have black trousers”) is outfit context, not a desired-product filter. A soft colour or “not too expensive” preference can rank verified candidates but cannot silently become a hard budget or replace an explicit Constraint. Numeric budgets are exact EGP Money. Currency is injected from the Storefront Definition (`currency: "EGP"` today); no conversion or multi-currency engine is in scope.
- Show two or three grounded options immediately for open-ended requests when possible, each with a short reason and an Exact Match / Alternative / Styling Suggestion label. Ask about occasion/style only when needed; do not pad the list to three with irrelevant items.
- Ordinary additions, quantity changes, and one-line removal are **Reversible Mutations**: optimistic update plus a ten-second exact Undo, not a confirmation modal. Explicit bulk “empty cart” and checkout submission are **Guarded Mutations**: fresh, specific, single-use Confirmation. The Shopper personally enters login, one-time code, and fictional card fields. Payment remains outside the Copilot.
- A locate request Spotlights an element and stops; an explicit open request navigates. Page text is untrusted data, never policy authority. Off-origin navigation, Sensitive Field reading/typing, duplicate/stale Actions, uncertain replay, and provider-driven browser authority stay blocked.

## Architecture actually used

Five conceptual parts communicate through versioned contracts:

| Part                  | Responsibility                                                                                                                                                                                  | Useful entry points                                                                                                  |
| --------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Controlled Storefront | Express/TypeScript, semantic Arabic RTL pages, fictional product truth and public catalogue endpoint, URL filters, minimal test-seeded cart, guarded fake checkout, account/order demonstration | `store/src/app.ts`, `store/src/views.ts`, `store/src/catalogue.ts`, `store/src/guarded-cart.ts`                      |
| Bridge                | Browser-side semantic Snapshot, stable page-local element IDs, one Action at a time, DOM execution, origin/sensitivity guards, action ledger                                                    | `bridge/src/snapshot.ts`, `bridge/src/actions.ts`, `bridge/src/runtime.ts`                                           |
| Panel                 | Shopper conversation, suggestions, status/Stop, Spotlight display, session recovery, iframe-to-Bridge messaging                                                                                 | `panel/src/main.ts`, `panel/src/panel.ts`, `panel/src/styles.css`                                                    |
| Agent                 | FastAPI service, provider-neutral Intent Interpreter, deterministic Intent Boundary/planner, catalogue verifier, exact Confirmation ledger, task/session events and recovery                    | `agent/app.py`, `agent/llm/`, `agent/catalogue.py`, `agent/confirmation.py`, `agent/planner.py`, `agent/sessions.py` |
| Evaluation Runner     | Scripted-model browser path, isolated Storefront reset, assertions against visible browser/Storefront state, opt-in live-model reports                                                          | `eval/cases.py`, `eval/runner.py`, `eval/services.py`, `eval/tests/`                                                 |

Typical path: Shopper text in Panel → Agent session → one model call for versioned Structured Intent → deterministic schema/source/currency/Storefront-vocabulary validation → (simple exact filters: canonical URL; semantic goal: read-only catalogue facts and verified options) → Agent Action event → Panel/Bridge → Storefront DOM/navigation → fresh Snapshot/Action Result → Agent verifies observed state before declaring success. The Intent Interpreter receives the current Shopper message, trusted Storefront Definition, bounded resolved context, and pending clarification—not raw page Snapshot or the entire conversation. The model does not choose arbitrary product IDs or directly click elements.

`storefront-definition.json` supplies known routes, capabilities, vocabulary, and EGP configuration. Product fixtures currently live in **`store/src/catalogue.ts`**, not the JSON/YAML file suggested by an older design. The read-only `GET /__catalogue/v1/products` is backed by those fixtures; `agent/catalogue.py` parses it and evaluates facts. There is no Redis, PostgreSQL, vector store, duplicated Agent-side product array, external merchant DB, or AWS runtime in the local MVP. Category pages use URL-addressable filters; the Bridge can fall back to visible controls if expected state does not materialize. Product detail pages use stable `/p/{product_id}` IDs rather than brittle search text.

The v1 Snapshot/Action/Action Result wire contracts are mirrored manually in Python and TypeScript and checked against `protocol/v1/fixtures`. A Snapshot is a capped semantic representation (12 kB), not a DOM dump. The Agent runs one Action at a time, up to eight steps. Session events use SSE; browser actions use `postMessage` with checked origins. Stop and task/action IDs prevent late, stale, or duplicate effects. Refresh reconnects and reconciles against a fresh Snapshot; an uncertain Action is not automatically replayed. Session state is in the Agent process, with a 30-minute inactivity TTL and a one-tab execution lease; Agent **process restarts are not durable**. The Panel recovers from a lost 404/410 session by starting a new one. Cart state is intended to outlive Copilot task state once cart work exists.

Ticket 10 adds a separate guarded route: explicit Shopper mutation request → Structured Intent v4 with current source → shared deterministic validation → current guarded-button Snapshot → localized confirmation card → exact one-use offer → confirmed `guarded_click` with task, target, cart revision, and effect → Bridge recheck and Shopper-filled form submit → Storefront token/revision consume → fresh page verification. Opening a destination remains normal navigation. A changed cart, sixty-second expiry, refresh, mismatched answer, or uncertain result cannot silently replay or mutate. The present cart state lives only in the Storefront process and is populated by a test-only seed until Ticket 11; it does not yet implement user-facing item add/Undo.

## What we tried, what failed, and what changed

These are historical findings, not claims that the current build still has every bug:

1. **Early scripted “brain” and fragile local startup.** The first examples were pattern/deterministic flows, not a connected LLM. `pnpm` was initially missing for the owner and `pnpm store` invoked pnpm's package-store command rather than the app script. Use `corepack pnpm run store`. When services were not running, port 4100 refused connections or the Panel said “Reconnecting…”. After Agent restarts, an old in-memory session ID caused a 404/410 recovery issue; Panel recovery was repaired. Test service processes are temporary, not deployment.
2. **NVIDIA model exploration failed the evidence gate.** Retired model IDs caused HTTP 410/404. Super improved with prompt and deterministic currency/budget guards but remained far below the exactness/safety gate (v4: 54/104 exact, 31/66 critical-safety exact, six HTTP failures). Ultra was unstable (v4: 93/104 HTTP failures). Suggested Llama 70B replacements were retired or returned 404. Do not “try Ultra again” as if a successful isolated response overrules the benchmark. Reports and dates are in `PROJECT_CHECKPOINT.md` and ignored `eval/reports/` outputs.
3. **Groq was more useful, but not certified.** Qwen 27B schema/safety viability was 9/9, exact 7/9, and repeatedly invented unstated Constraints. GPT-OSS 120B looked better (initial 8/9 exact; a further 12/18 exact and 17/18 safe) but followed an Arabic injected checkout instruction. The Intent Boundary was tightened against navigation mixed with discovery Constraints and prompt override. The owner knowingly pinned GPT-OSS 120B for **local development only**, with the strict gate deferred to Bedrock. Groq free-tier HTTP 429 and interpretation delays are real throughput limits; a rotated key once restored testing, and staged status now explains throttling/network/timeout/invalid output rather than a generic unexplained Retry. Do not put an API key in code or this handoff.
4. **Literal filters were not a smart copilot.** A request for hiking shoes with grip or a brown shirt could silently lose needs and show unrelated results. The zero-silent-drop Intent Boundary now asks/declines when recognized requirements cannot be represented. A visible result count is checked before claiming success; zero and unverified counts get honest messages. Fixing this solely by adding whole Shopper phrases to a keyword list was rejected. Semantic paraphrase belongs to the LLM; facts, currency, allowed destinations, and Action safety stay deterministic.
5. **The first three owner-written browser requests exposed a deeper grounding failure.** Summer shirt got an honest zero but no alternatives; wedding shoes degraded to broad black shoes and falsely claimed a match, dropping formal/leather/price preference; street-running workouts asked a generic clarification. The approved read-only catalogue interface, explicit `features`/`suitable_for`/`wear_position`, sourced requirements, deterministic Exact Match/Alternative verification, and language-independent completion assertion repaired this. The unchanged requests later passed with actual product IDs and labelled gaps; see evidence below. No database or vector system was added.
6. **Football filter existed in UI but not the authoritative Definition.** `عايز كوتشي كرة اكسر بيه الدنيا مقاسي 43` previously produced size-only shoes. The Definition and Intent Boundary now recognize the football type, and the unchanged phrase passed a live Groq browser run with four filtered products. The lesson is that UI affordances alone are not Storefront capability authority.
7. **Navigation and follow-ups were too literal.** “Where is the cart?” should Spotlight, while “open it for me” after a verified Spotlight should navigate. An ambiguous cart/account choice once failed because the Bridge's Action sequence was not registered correctly; that was repaired without relaxing stale/duplicate rejection. The follow-up context is bounded and single-use, not a full conversation transcript. The sign-in `Continue` path formerly looped on an unchanged login page; now it waits for the Shopper's own authentication and resumes only after a fresh authenticated Snapshot. Egyptian open-verb variants and a prompt example improved understanding, but vocabulary is not intended as an exhaustive sentence parser.
8. **Outfit reasoning was overly special-cased.** A green T-shirt/brown trousers outfit originally led to a category question or all shoes rather than grounded suggestions. Structured Intent v3 separates browse/recommend/style, multiple owned items and soft palette preferences; clarification preserves the original goal; a generic catalogue style evaluator replaced the black-trousers-only rule. Review then corrected source-span and colour-explanation edge cases. Seven live cases and a login follow-up passed afterward, but that is finite evidence, not universal language competence.
9. **Recommended product page follow-up used brittle text search.** `طب ينفع توريني صفحة كوتشي ممشى النيل ده` once searched the literal phrase and reported no match. The Storefront now has `/p/{product_id}` and the Agent may open only a prior verified suggestion ID with current Shopper source. An ambiguous unnamed reference asks which item; an invented ID is rejected. The exact two-turn live browser flow passed after a 429/key-rotation interruption.
10. **Ticket 09 found an origin-check bypass.** An explicit `navigate` was already guarded, but clicking an off-origin link or submitting an external form was not. The new guard and direct/browser regressions closed those paths. Dynamic field sensitivity, sensitive label leakage, and disabled-control false success were also found by failing tests and repaired. A basic disabled UI alone was not treated as sufficient stock authority; the Storefront endpoint now rejects unavailable IDs too.
11. **Ticket 10 found a direct-import cycle.** The full Agent suite imported `agent.llm` before `agent.confirmation`, masking a circular package import. Running `test_confirmation.py` alone failed during collection. The confirmation module now imports intent types only for static typing and loads the scripted fixture type lazily; standalone and full runs pass. A separate session-boundary regression also caught a bypass where a direct interpreter result could claim a negated bulk mutation; the shared validator now rejects it at both seams.
12. **Live model availability remains different from deterministic safety.** Groq recognized `Empty my cart` and `فضي السلة` in two opt-in probes on the current prompt, but a repeat returned HTTP 429. No live fictional-checkout browser acceptance is claimed. The local scripted browser suite proves the Action boundary and simulated checkout path, not free-tier uptime or arbitrary language accuracy.

## Assumptions: true, false, and still unproven

| Assumption                                                                        | Current judgment and evidence                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| “The model alone can safely plan browser Actions.”                                | **False.** Groq's Arabic injection and dropped Constraints required deterministic Intent/Action boundaries. The model only interprets; code authorizes.                                                                                                       |
| “A nonzero Storefront search result proves the Shopper's goal is met.”            | **False.** Black shoes did not prove formal leather wedding suitability. ## Operational notes and known traps

- The app has three local services: Controlled Storefront `http://localhost:4000/`, Agent health `http://127.0.0.1:8000/health`, Panel `http://localhost:4100/`. The Panel embeds the Storefront; opening only 4100 without the other services gives a broken frame or “Reconnecting…”. Ticket 10 browser tests start and stop their own services, so **do not assume services remain running** after verification. Development startup now binds these services to loopback for the local-MVP threat boundary.
- Start the Storefront with `corepack pnpm run store`, **not** `pnpm store`. Start the Agent with `.venv\Scripts\python.exe -m uvicorn agent.app:app --host 127.0.0.1 --port 8000`, and the Panel with `corepack pnpm panel`. Confirm ports before starting another copy. Existing demo services on fixed ports can cause `eval/services.py` to connect to the **wrong, live Groq process** while its scripted test processes fail to bind; that previously turned a safety test into a 429. For isolated browser evaluation, identify the exact project-owned processes, stop them safely, run the scripted suite, then restore the demo services and check all endpoints.
- The declared Python target is **3.12** (`pyproject.toml`); `.venv` reports 3.12.5. The shell's `python` currently reports 3.13.14. `corepack pnpm test` did pass with that shell Python on 2026-09-25, but use `.venv\Scripts\python.exe` for supported local Python checks and Ruff. Node/pnpm versions are pinned in `package.json`.
- Test/build commands: `corepack pnpm test`, `corepack pnpm build`, `corepack pnpm lint:ts`, `.venv\Scripts\python.exe -m ruff check agent eval`, `.venv\Scripts\python.exe -m ruff format --check agent eval`, and `git diff --check`. `pnpm lint` can select a system Python without Ruff, so the equivalent project-venv checks may be needed. Repository-wide Prettier has historically flagged unrelated older files; check changed files and report that distinction honestly.
- `eval/services.py` defaults to the scripted provider. Live Groq runs require deliberate opt-in and may hit the free-tier request/token window; pacing is needed for meaningful semantic evaluation. Never mistake “HTTP 429” for a failed product interpretation, or a scripted pass for a live model pass.
- The Storefront and Panel are still visually plain. Ticket 11B, not an ad hoc CSS change during a safety ticket, is the approved presentation polish. Product detail pages exist; available products still cannot be added. The payment form is fictional and intentionally Shopper-operated.
- Ticket 10's cart is seeded only through `POST /__test/cart` with known available product IDs (for example `shoe-09`) so the guarded flow can be demonstrated before Ticket 11. `/__test/cart-state` exposes only fictional in-memory state. An empty cart disables fictional checkout. The local registration endpoint accepts confirmation tokens only at the Controlled Storefront and is deliberately not a production security boundary; do not expose it via a public host or deploy it unchanged.
- The architecture review at `architecture-review.md` proposed deepening the Shopping Task, Storefront Definition, and Panel seams. The owner approved **incremental implementation within active tickets**, not prerequisite refactors that block a working browser flow. Keep focused direct safety tests alongside browser tests.
- The AWS research in `docs/research/2026-09-23-aws-shopping-copilot-options.md` recommends a narrow Bedrock Runtime `Converse` adapter, not managed Agents as action authority, and a low-cost retrieval baseline before any vector service. It is research dated 2026-09-23, **not** a provisioned AWS environment or current-price promise. Recheck Region/model/credit eligibility/pricing before spending the roughly $200 credits. No AWS resource is known to have been created by this project work.
